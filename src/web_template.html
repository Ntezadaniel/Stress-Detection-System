Read this before you judge the demo

  • This is a replay of recorded data from three wearers held out of training, not a live sensor.
  • Inference times are JavaScript on this device. They are a different runtime from the PyTorch numbers in the thesis, and neither was measured on a Raspberry Pi or ESP32.
  • The alert rule (two stress windows in a row) is illustrative and was not part of the evaluation.
  • Three wearers are a small sample. Quote the cross-validated numbers on the Results tab, not this replay.
  • Names are editable placeholders. WESAD subjects are anonymised, so no real person can be identified from this data. The integrity tag shows that signals, wearer ID and device ID were bound together and not altered; it does not prove who physically wore the device.
  • Decision support only. This is not a medical, diagnostic or disciplinary tool.

Why per-wearer normalisation matters

Teacher modelAccuracyStress F1
Global normalisation0.530.55
Per-wearer normalisation0.690.81

People differ in resting heart rate, skin conductance and skin temperature. Standardising each wearer against their own data removed most of that noise. In a real device this needs a short calibration period.

Limitations

  • Only 15 subjects, so fold-to-fold variation is large (accuracy standard deviation about 0.1).
  • Amusement remains the weakest class for every model.
  • Latency was measured on development hardware, not on a Raspberry Pi Zero or ESP32. This still has to be verified on the target device.
  • Results come from one public lab dataset (WESAD). Performance on real factory workers is untested.

The problem: the AI models that detect stress best are too heavy for the cheap, low-power wearables available to many employers. The idea: let a large teacher model teach a much smaller student, so the accurate model can run on the device itself.

The pipeline

1. Wrist sensorsPulse (BVP), skin conductance (EDA), skin temperature and movement.
2. 60-second windowsThe stream is cut into one-minute windows, 4 Hz, four channels.
3. Per-wearer calibrationSignals are standardised against that person’s own baseline.
4. Student model on device59,923 parameters. Classifies baseline, stress or amusement.
5. Level, ID and tagOnly the stress level, a pseudonymous wearer ID and an integrity tag are sent. Raw signals stay on the wearer.

Knowledge distillation

A large teacher (221,251 parameters) is trained first. A student with 72.9% fewer parameters then learns from the teacher’s full probability output, not just the right/wrong label, plus the true label itself. That extra information is why the small model can match or beat its teacher on a small dataset.

Reinforced means a cost-sensitive penalty: during training a missed stress case counts five times more than other mistakes, because failing to notice real stress is worse than a false alarm.

What this page demonstrates

  • The trained teacher and student weights are embedded in this page and run in JavaScript, so it works offline and on a phone.
  • A built-in check compares the browser output with the original PyTorch output (see the badge on the Live demo tab).
  • The wearer and normalisation settings change the data pipeline live, so you can see the calibration requirement for yourself.

Ethics and intended use

The system is designed as decision support: it surfaces an indicator to a supervisor or occupational-health practitioner, who keeps authority over any response. It should not be used for discipline or automated decisions. Data stays on the device and only a stress level, a pseudonymous wearer ID and an integrity tag are transmitted. Names are held in a separate enrolment record and shown only to the authorised practitioner, with the wearer’s consent. The research data (WESAD) was collected and anonymised by its original authors under their own ethical approval.
